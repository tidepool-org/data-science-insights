# forecast_uncertainty — Architecture

## Current state (as of 2026-09-08)

**Where the project stands.** Prediction intervals for a deterministic glucose forecast, built and evaluated on the cohort-B behavior traces with real therapy settings, at 13 horizons (5 … 30 / 60 / 90 / 120 / 180 / 240 / 300 / 360 min). The interval is `predicted + location(state, h) + scale(state, h) × [q_lo, q_hi]_h`; both models share ONE structure (horizon-interacted features + hour terms) and one candidate set per forecaster: the forecaster's own FORECAST TERMS (what it predicted: Loop's displayed change, or the reconstructed carb / insulin components; none for persistence) plus five shared STATE features (IOB × ISF, glucose level, prior 30-min change, recent carbs and bolus in mg/dL). Lower bound floored at 40 mg/dL. Evaluated by PIT / CRPS under temporal and leave-one-user-out splits, a nested-spec comparison with conditional coverage, a model LADDER from per-horizon constants to the full model drawn on one real day (figure 19) and summarized under leave-one-user-out for several forecasters at once (figure 20), and an online adaptive level.

**Findings that shape the next steps.** The object of comparison is now Loop's DISPLAYED forecast (the app's `bgForecast`) against persistence; the reconstructed forecasters remain provisional until the controller-insulin export lands. (1) As a point forecast Loop beats persistence only in the first 10–25 minutes and is 4–9% worse beyond, with a systematic low bias growing to 30–40 mg/dL by 3–6 h; its predicted change correlates with the realized change (0.3–0.5), and a two-parameter recalibration turns that into a 6–11% win over persistence. (2) In the interval model Loop's forecast term earns a place: one term recovers 2.8 CRPS over constants for every held-out user, where IOB × ISF on persistence recovers 0.4. (3) With the full shared state set the two forecasters converge (LOUO CRPS 17.0 vs 17.4; 180-min widths 175 vs 177 on the ladder day): the forecast term and glucose level are largely substitutes, because most of Loop's long-horizon correlation is mean reversion seen through IOB (partial correlation given level 0.08 at 6 h, 0.43 at 5 min). (4) Predicted rises remain under-covered with Loop (0.91 vs 0.95) at every rung; scale-side variants do not touch it. (5) The scale model is invisible to pooled CRPS and matters for conditional (post-meal) coverage only. (6) The dose-magnitude terms (IOB × ISF, recent bolus) can leave the LOCATION at negligible cost (LOUO 16.93 vs 16.98 for Loop, within run-to-run noise; +0.07 on persistence; coverage unchanged) — `location_no_insulin`, the ladder's last rung, is the spec for anything that will sweep a dose. Not FDA-scoped. Dates and numbers in project_history.md.

**Operational rule:** the residual tables are several GB in memory; fold workers load only `evaluation_columns(out_dir)`, cap training rows at 400k, and run two at a time — a six-worker sweep rebooted the machine on 2026-09-06 (see history). **Loop's decisions through the interval (Level A, 2026-09-06):** at decisions where Loop recommends insulin, the 3-h 95% floor is below 70 at about a quarter (series) / 38% (bolus-time) of decisions and ranks the hypos that followed better than Loop's own forecast minimum (AUC 0.70 vs 0.64); titration against the floor needs a mechanistic dose channel first (`interval_in_the_loop.md`). **Proposed (2026-09-08, awaiting approval):** one origin-state taxonomy for every evaluation and figure — intervention state (post-carb early / late, post-bolus no carb, quiet) × glucose level band × trend — replacing the per-figure event windows and the forecaster-dependent predicted rise / fall (history entry of 2026-09-08). **Next (user's order):** how interventions move the location and scale — figures 24–25 show it on one event and as the median path: the scale steps up at the entry (+27% / +24% / +4% at 30 / 60 / 180 min, scaling with the bolus in glucose units) while the location builds over the following hour to +17 / +39 mg/dL at 60 / 180 min as Loop's IOB-driven predicted fall grows; next the systematic version across all events — before any per-user adaptation; the predicted-rise under-coverage; the controller-insulin export for the reconstructed forecasters.

Run everything from the project root: `python run_residuals.py --forecaster <name> --out-dir <dir>`, then `python evaluation/<script>.py --out-dir <dir>`; the evaluation scripts and tests put the project root on `sys.path` themselves. `model/` and `evaluation/` are packages.

**Memory rule for the evaluation scripts.** Measure one fold worker before choosing `--jobs` (`/usr/bin/time -l` → maximum resident set size; 6.1 GB per worker on 2026-09-06), keep jobs × peak under ~40% of RAM (two workers on the 36 GB laptop), keep `--max-train-rows 400000`, cap BLAS threads at 2 per worker, run stages strictly one after another (`outputs_sweep/chain_sweep.sh` is the template), and run nothing else heavy alongside.

## Data

Cohort-B traces: `behavior_model/data/behavior_traces_b/{users,cgm,carbs,boluses,dosing}.csv` (pseudonymized ids; continuous per-user windows; 5-min tick contract from behavior_model §6). Known limits: no basal / autobolus / settings columns; `cob` all-NaN until the pending COB re-export; Loop forecasts not exported (re-export with `bgForecast` is the drop-in path).

## Caveats carried into every result
- Residuals are against realized glucose under closed-loop operation (controller intervention is inside the residual; autoboluses are not in the export, so it cannot be corrected).
- Insulin history = user-initiated boluses only; scheduled basal treated as neutral (Loop convention). The controller's temp-basal deviations and autoboluses are NOT in the forecast (median +0.4 U of a typical 1.3 U displayed IOB; −0.9 to +3.0 U across origins) and are strongly glucose-dependent, so the static forecast is biased low when glucose is low and high when it is high for reasons unrelated to its curves. Fix pending: export the basal and automated-bolus streams.
- Carb absorption time is Loop's 180-min default for every entry: the export's `absorption_minutes` column is empty.
- Timezone: one per-user offset (`tz_offset_min`), applied once in `run_residuals.py`; the stream-timing QC figure (meals peak at local breakfast / lunch) confirms it.
